# Scarcity & Urgency Ideas

> Part of the [[00 - Secret Sauce Index|Secret Sauce]] course. See also [[Sales MOC]] · [[Marketing MOC]].

## 3 Scarcity Ideas

### 1) Limited Supply of Seats/Slots

This is the most common type of scarcity because almost always we just cannot accommodate everyone in a short span of time.

For example in your business, you might only have 489 stocks left in your inventory or if you're selling service, you might only be able to accommodate 18 clients this month right?

In reality, it's not possible to accommodate the world based on your fulfillment today. That means, scarcity and limited seats or slots are already happening in your business.

However, not a lot of entrepreneurs communicate this to your customers. The act of just communicating increases the likelihood of buying.

Now, you don't need a lot of sales before using this. You can communicate this with your clients even if you don't have a single sale yet.

You can say...

> "Sir, we only ordered 100 stocks for this month and we don't know when the next batch of inventory will arrive yet".

You're just stating facts. But people take action NOW because there's limited supply.

People buy through emotions. Having a limit increases desire because of FOMO (Fear of Missing Out).

### 2) Limited Supply of Bonuses

This is similar with #1, but the difference here is you're intentionally putting a limit to the bonuses or freebies you're giving away.

For example, if you're selling a digital product like an ebook. You can't say there's only 1,000 copies because an e-book doesn't have any limitation right?

However, what you can say is…

> "If you're one of the first 20 people to buy my ebook today, I'm also gonna give you my '30-Day Meal Plan' worth P999 for FREE! I want to reward fast action-takers because they are the people who are most likely to succeed in implementing my program."

Alright, so if you noticed...

The 30-Day Meal Plan is also a digital product and is also "unlimited" in nature.

However, I gave a reason why I'm limiting it to only 20 slots, which is it's my way of rewarding fast-action takers.

So even if your product is unlimited in nature, you can still create scarcity by intentionally limiting your supply and explaining why you're doing that so that your customers will understand.

### 3) Never Available Again

This is one of the most powerful scarcity techniques.

This explains why collectibles sky rocket through the roof.

Have you noticed antiques, old paintings, old cars are worth millions of dollars even if in reality you're not gonna use it anyways?

It's because it's now almost impossible to replicate or create a similar product or output today.

And since it's rare and only 4 people have this in the world... It creates extreme demand because it's never available again.

## 4 Urgency Ideas

### 1) Event-Based Urgency

This is very useful for events or classes that don't happen that often.

For example, if you have a summit that happens once a year. That's urgency right there. Because if they don't attend or sign-up, they would have to wait for another year to join again.

Or if you have a masterclass that happens every quarter, if they don't sign-up now, they would have to wait for the next batch.

Of course, the lesser the frequency, the more powerful it is. Meaning, once a year is more powerful than every quarter.

The longer the time they would have to wait for the next batch, the more likely they would take action now. Because if they don't take action, there's pain involved. The pain of waiting for the next batch or event.

### 2) Seasonal Urgency

This is perfectly implemented by Shopee and Lazada in their monthly sale.

Remember? 1:1 sale, 2:2 sale, 3:3 sale, 4:4 sale, etc.

Every month there's a sale! But... There's a reason for the sale. In this case, it's because of the number.

Because if the sale was like 1:26 sale, 2:13 sales... It doesn't make any sense. So that sale should have a strong reason why. In this example, it's because of the Season.

For example:

- January - New Year's Sale
- February - Valentines Sale
- March - Summer Sale
- And so on…

So the urgency happens every month but there's a new reason for the sale. Because if it's just the same thing with no reason, it will not have its power.

### 3) Pricing or Bonus Based Urgency

I learned this when my partner was working with Toyota selling cars.

Almost every month, they have new promos and freebies and they don't really know what will be the promo in the succeeding months.

Since buying a car is a high ticket purchase and most people "wait" for the right time, car sales agents communicate the urgency of their promo.

They usually say…

> "Sir, we have a promo now. Instead of paying a downpayment of P120k, you can get this brand new Toyota Wigo for just a P50k downpayment. However, we don't know when this promo will last because our company changes our promo every month"

Another angle could be…

> "Sir, we have a promo now. Our tint upgrade cost P10,000 if you buy it separately.
>
> However, our company is giving a promo that you're gonna get it for FREE and you buy a car today. Our company changes our promo once in a while without informing us ahead of time. So take this offer before it goes away"

### 4) Losing Time & Opportunity Urgency

I first learned this strategy when I was in networking marketing.

In my first few weeks as a member... After my presentation, I would just tell my prospect to contact me when they're already ready to join.

7 years later, not a single one came back to me to join or buy my product.

I then learned from my mentor to use these lines during my close…

> "The results that you're getting now are based on the decision you made 5 years ago. And the results you're gonna have 5 years from now, is based on the decision you'll make today…
>
> If you decide tomorrow or next week... You've already lost. You lost time and opportunity.
>
> By the time that you've decided tomorrow, other people have already gotten started.
>
> It's like running a marathon, but you only started running a day after.
>
> If you get started now, you're already ahead of most people and I believe that the early bird catches the worm."

So what you're using here is the lost time and opportunity if they will still think about it or delay taking action.
