# Crafting Irresistible Offers: A Step-By-Step Guide

> Part of the [[00 - Secret Sauce Index|Secret Sauce]] course. See also [[Sales MOC]] · [[Marketing MOC]].

Every successful business is built on the foundation of a strong offer. An offer isn't just a product or service—it's the promise of a solution to a problem, the pathway to achieving a dream, or the tool to make life easier.

This will guide you through the process of crafting offers that your customers can't resist.

By understanding and applying the principles outlined here, you can create offers that stand out in the market and drive your business growth.

Introducing the $100M Offer Formula by Alex Hormozi:

> Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

## 1) Dream Outcome

This represents the ultimate result or transformation the customer desires. The bigger or more desirable the dream outcome, the more valuable the offer.

**Example:** We had an offer that gave returns on a 2-year time window. However, we were wondering why people weren't getting our offer even though the returns were crazy. So apply the $100M Offer Formula, we increased the dream outcome from just 2 years to 20 years and the conversions skyrocketed!

## 2) Perceived Likelihood of Achievement

This is how confident the customer feels that they will achieve the dream outcome with your offer. The higher the perceived likelihood, the more valuable the offer.

**Example:** We introduced a ₱500,000 mastermind program—a significant investment, no doubt. Despite the high price, our clients eagerly joined. Why? Because we guaranteed that if they didn't at least double their profit within 12 months, we would refund every peso.

This powerful guarantee demonstrated our confidence in the program (certainty) and assured our clients that they were making a secure and worthwhile investment in their business.

## 3) Time Delay

This refers to the amount of time it takes for the customer to achieve the dream outcome. The shorter the time delay, the higher the perceived value.

**Example:** Imagine you're offering a weight loss program. The dream outcome for your customer is to lose 10 pounds. If you offer a program that helps them achieve this in just 4 weeks instead of the usual 12 weeks, the perceived value of your program increases significantly.

**Example Pitch:** "With our 4-week accelerated weight loss program, you can lose 10 pounds in just one month, rather than waiting 3 months or more with other programs."

By reducing the time delay, you're making the outcome more immediate, which enhances the appeal and value of your offer.

## 4) Effort and Sacrifice

This includes the work, inconvenience, or costs the customer must endure to achieve the outcome. The less effort and sacrifice required, the more valuable the offer.

**Example:** Initially, we offered our comprehensive marketing program for just P10,000. It was a complete package covering every marketing course a business owner could wish for.

However, we discovered that business owners often lack the time to engage with all these DIY (do-it-yourself) courses.

So, we reimagined our approach. Instead of focusing on the business owner alone, we shifted our messaging to provide training and access to their entire sales and marketing team.

This change allowed us to enhance the value of our offer, leading us to successfully sell the program for P250,000. And business owners were happy to pay for it!

In summary, to create a high-value offer, you want to maximize the Dream Outcome and Perceived Likelihood of Achievement while minimizing the Time Delay, Effort, and Sacrifice.

This formula can be used to evaluate and optimize your offers to make them more compelling to potential customers.
