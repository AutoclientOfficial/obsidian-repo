# Target Market: Understanding Your Audience For Greater Sales

> Part of the [[00 - Secret Sauce Index|Secret Sauce]] course. See also [[Sales MOC]] · [[Marketing MOC]].

To boost your sales, you need to understand the market where you're selling. This part will help you learn about the market and how it affects your sales.

We'll look at how to figure out who your ideal customers are, what they want, and what your competitors are doing. By knowing these things, you can create better sales strategies that speak directly to your customers' needs and make them more likely to buy from you.

Understanding your market is key to attracting more leads and turning them into customers.

To illustrate the importance of understanding your market, consider this excerpt from The Boron Letters by Gary Halbert *(one of the top copywriters of all time)*.

> As you know, once in a while I give a class on copywriting and/or selling by mail. One of the questions I like to ask my students is: "If you and I both owned a hamburger stand and we were in a contest to see who would sell the most hamburgers, what advantages would you most like to have on your side?"
>
> The answers vary. Some people say they would like to have the advantage of having superior meat from which to make their hamburgers. Others say they want sesame seed buns. Others mention location. Someone usually wants to be able to offer the lowest prices.
>
> And so on.
>
> Anyway, after my students are finished telling what advantages they would most like to have I say to them: "O.K., I'll give you every single advantage you asked for. I, myself, only want one advantage and, if you will give it to me, I will whip the pants off of all of you when it comes to selling burgers!"
>
> "What advantage do you want?" they ask.
>
> "The only advantage I want," I reply, "is A STARVING CROWD!"
>
> Think about it.

The key takeaway from this story is that no matter how good your product is, its success heavily depends on where and how you market it. The student who chose the prime location understood that the market's dynamics—where you place your stand, who your customers are, and how you reach them—can significantly impact your success.

In other words, understanding your market means knowing where your customers are, what they need, and how you can stand out from your competitors. By focusing on these aspects, you can better position your product and make strategic decisions that will lead to higher sales.

That's why when it comes to choosing the right market for your products and services, you look for a market that NOT only is a starving crowd but must have these 2 important qualities:

- Willing to buy
- Able to buy

## Case Studies

### Case Study: Teaching Online Marketing

- Employees Who Wants to Start an Online Business
- Offline Business Owners Who Wants to Market Online

We had a program teaching our target market how to do online marketing and making money online. The first audience were employees. Yes, they may be willing to start an online business, but they aren't necessarily able to afford the program.

When we shifted our market to existing business owners who are currently doing business offline and introduced a new way of earning via online, these markets are definitely willing and able to afford our programs.

### Case Study: Teaching Forex Trading

- Earn Additional Income
- Invest & Retire

Another case study, where the offer was about learning how to do forex trading. The first audience definitely want (or willing) to learn about forex, but since their main motivation is about earning additional income, they definitely can not afford (or not able) to avail of the program yet.

While on the other hand, when the market was changed to people who already have the savings and want to invest and grow their money, they were a market who are willing and able to invest in the program.

### Case Study: Selling Business Opportunity

- Employees in the Philippines
- OFWs

Same scenario, we have a business opportunity program for employees in the Philippines. Again, they are willing but NOT able to invest in the opportunity.

While OFWs are definitely the market who are willing to have a business opportunity (so that they can finally go home for good in the Philippines) and are able to invest in the opportunity too.

That's what a starving crowd is all about!

Understand and master this, you'll definitely leave your competition behind.
