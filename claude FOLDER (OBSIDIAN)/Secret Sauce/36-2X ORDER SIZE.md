# Profit Multipliers: The 3B's to Double Your Order Size

> Part of the [[00 - Secret Sauce Index|Secret Sauce]] course. See also [[Sales MOC]] · [[Marketing MOC]].

Boosting the average order size is a powerful strategy to increase revenue without needing to attract more customers. Here, we'll explore how to effectively double each customer's order size using the 3B's framework: Bill, Bundle, and Better. By focusing on these key areas, you can maximize the value of every transaction and drive significant revenue growth.

## The Power of Increasing Order Size

Maximizing the value of each transaction is just as important as acquiring new customers. By increasing the average order size, you can boost your profits significantly without additional marketing costs. This approach involves encouraging customers to spend more during each purchase.

## The 3B's Framework for Doubling Your Order Size

### 1. Bill

**Definition**: Bill higher by increasing the price of your offer or by adding premium features and services.

**Strategy**:

- **Price Increases**: Adjust your pricing structure to reflect the added value of your products or services.
- **Premium Features**: Offer enhanced versions of your product with additional features or benefits.
- **Adding Bonuses**: Include valuable bonuses or extra incentives to justify a higher price and increase perceived value.

**Example**: If you sell a basic subscription service for P990, introduce a premium version with exclusive features at P4,990. Add bonuses like a free eBook or a month of free support to sweeten the deal and make the higher price more attractive.

### 2. Bundle

**Definition**: Combine related products or services into a single package at a discounted price to encourage customers to purchase more.

**Strategy**:

- **Product Bundling**: Offer a package deal that includes a primary product plus several complementary items.
- **Discounted Bundles**: Provide a discount for purchasing the bundle compared to buying each item separately.

**Example**: Create a "starter kit" for a fitness program that includes a workout guide, a meal plan, and a set of resistance bands, priced lower than purchasing each item individually.

### 3. Better

**Definition**: Offer something bigger and better by enhancing the value proposition of your offer.

**Strategy**:

- **Upsells**: Encourage customers to upgrade to a higher-end version of the product.
- **Exclusive Packages**: Provide exclusive offers or premium options that deliver greater value.

**Example**: If a customer is purchasing a standard car, offer them an upgrade to a luxury model with additional features and benefits, such as leather seats or advanced technology.

## Implementing the 3B's Framework

To effectively use the 3B's framework, follow these steps:

1. **Analyze Your Offerings**: Review your current products and services to identify opportunities for implementing the 3B's. Look for areas where you can increase pricing, bundle products, or offer premium options.

2. **Customize Your Approach**: Tailor the 3B's strategies to suit your specific market and customer needs. Ensure that your price increases, bundles, and premium offers are relevant and attractive to your target audience.

3. **Test and Optimize**: Continuously test different approaches to the 3B's. Analyze customer responses and sales data to refine your strategies and improve performance.

## Conclusion: Maximizing Customer Value with the 3B's

By focusing on **Bill, Bundle,** and **Better**, you can effectively increase the average order size and extract more value from each customer interaction. Implement these strategies thoughtfully and consistently, and watch as your business grows with each transaction.

## Other Strategies to Double Your Order Size

1. **Cross-Selling**
   - **Definition**: Cross-selling involves suggesting complementary products or services that enhance the value of the initial purchase.
   - **Example**: If a customer buys a smartphone, offering them a phone case or screen protector as an additional purchase.

2. **Upselling**
   - **Definition**: Upselling encourages customers to purchase a higher-end version of the product they are considering.
   - **Example**: If a customer is looking at a mid-range laptop, you could suggest the benefits of upgrading to a premium model with better features.

3. **Bundling**
   - **Definition**: Bundling combines related products or services at a discounted price, encouraging customers to buy more items together.
   - **Example**: Offering a "starter kit" that includes a primary product plus several essential accessories at a lower price than buying each item separately.

4. **Volume Discounts**
   - **Definition**: Volume discounts incentivize customers to purchase larger quantities by offering a reduced price for buying in bulk.
   - **Example**: "Buy two, get one free" deals or discounts on purchases of multiple items.

5. **Add-Ons and Upgrades**
   - **Definition**: Offer additional features, services, or extended warranties at the point of sale.
   - **Example**: When purchasing a car, offering an extended warranty or premium add-ons like leather seats or a sunroof.

6. **Limited-Time Offers**
   - **Definition**: Create a sense of urgency by offering time-limited deals that encourage customers to add more to their cart before checking out.
   - **Example**: "Add this product to your order within the next 15 minutes and get 10% off!"
