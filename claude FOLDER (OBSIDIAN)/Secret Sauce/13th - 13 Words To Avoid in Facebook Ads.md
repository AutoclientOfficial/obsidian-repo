# 13 Words to Avoid In Facebook Ads

> Part of the [[00 - Secret Sauce Index|Secret Sauce]] course. See also [[Sales MOC]] · [[Marketing MOC]].

### 1) Facebook

- The Facebook platform doesn't want other people to use its name. That means if your ads contain the word "Facebook", there's a high chance it will not be approved. Instead use generic words like "social media".

### 2) Get rich quick

- Under Facebook's Advertising Policy, it doesn't allow income opportunities that offer quick compensation for little investment. This has brought a negative impact to users where-in they found an ad online, invested in the opportunity, and eventually got scammed. Facebook wants to avoid this from happening.

### 3) Income/ Millions / Money

- Similar to #2, even if what you're selling isn't a get rich quick opportunity, but if you're using the words income, millions, money, etc., it is associated with making quick money. Instead, hide these words using other similar words with the same meaning. For example, instead of using "income", I use the word "sales". Instead of using the word "money", I use the word "blue bills".

### 4) You

- Facebook doesn't want your ads to be directly said to the user because it affects the user experience. Usually phrases that use the word "You" is something that's direct. Instead, make the word first person (I or me) or third person (he, she, them). For example, instead of saying… Are you broke for years?, convert it to phrases like… "I was broke for years..."

### 5) Cure

- Under the Facebook Ad Policy, content that's misleading, deceptive, or unrealistic is one of the grounds for disapproval and termination of the account. So if you have supplements that you're promoting that may or may not cure a disease, I suggest to avoid using the word "cure" because in the eyes of Facebook, you're promising something that may not be legally proven. Instead of using "This will cure…", you can rephrase it to "This will help solve…"

### 6) ? (Question Mark)

- Under #4, I shared that Facebook doesn't want you to create ads that's direct. Aside from using "you", most people use a question in their ads that's also a form of direct confrontation. So instead of using a question like… "Tired of your job?" convert the phrase to a sentence talking about yourself or other people like… "After working 9-5 for 5 years, I was exhausted…"

### 7) Financial

- Facebook doesn't want you to discriminate against a person's personal attributes like age, gender, disabilities, or financial status. Using words like Financial Freedom or Financially Abundant is a violation of this advertising policy in the eyes of Facebook because you're judging a person's financial status.

### 8) [Benefit] in [Timeframe]

- Similar to #5, Facebook doesn't want advertisers to run ads that's misleading or deceptive. Putting a timeframe like "Lose 3 kg in 10 days" may be realistic, however in the eyes of Facebook, it's a misleading promise because what's applicable with one person might not be applicable with others. I agree that adding a timeframe will appeal to more people, however, having an active ad account that can run for a lifetime is more important than getting a quick buck that will only last for a few days.

### 9) Make Money Online/ Work From Home

- Similar to #2, creating ads that have the words "Make Money Online" or "Work From Home" is flagged by Facebook as get-rich-quick opportunities. It doesn't mean that you're no longer allowed to promote your product. You can instead replace these phrases using not so obvious words. For example: Replace "make money online" into "generate sales using a mobile phone", replace "work from home" into "work from your own living room"

### 10) Fat/ Anxiety/ Fear

- Words that are similar to "fat, anxiety, fear, depression, etc." fall under "sensitive topics" which are closely monitored by Facebook. This is a violation under the Personal Attributes Policy. Instead of using negative words, use words that provide positivity and happiness. For example, instead of using "Lose your fats this summer!", convert it to… "Become fit & confident this summer!"

### 11) "&%#%^@%"

- Most probably, you might have noticed that there aren't curse words in traditional media like TV, Radio, Newspaper. That's similar to Facebook or any other social media platform. Even if you hide the curse words by using punctuation, these can still be tracked by Facebook's smart algorithm. Instead, totally avoid using them. If you want to deliver the emotion of a message, using emojis is the best option.

### 12) Caps Lock Words

- Using all caps like "STOP SCROLLING & CLICK HERE!!!" is a violation of the policy that falls under "Grammar & Profanity". Having multiple capital letters or using a lot of exclamation points is like shouting at Facebook's users. You might think writing in caps lock is more visible to the users but in reality it's making the users annoyed. Use caps lock at a few words only that need emphasis or you can use emojis instead to deliver the emotion of your message.

### 13) Guarantee

- Similar to #5, using the word "guarantee" is another misleading claim. It's a promise that may be applicable by one person but not everyone. For example: "Make P100k in this business guaranteed", "Get whiter & brighter, guaranteed results". You can mention the benefits or the results of your product but just avoid the word "Guarantee"
